## 1. Front page

**The view in three sentences.** Sanofi is paying $41.0bn [Announced] for assets that are worth $7.3bn on the book's own method and $18.1bn on margins more generous to a bought asset [Overlay base]; neither figure gets near the price, so the deal destroys $9 to $14 of value per ADR against a standalone book value of $68.17 [Book]. The stock still screens cheap: even after that charge the pro forma value of $54.24 per ADR [Overlay base] sits 16% above the $46.77 close of 23 November [Book], and the book's three-layer bear case is $34.72. Our view is that the standalone value is overstated by a future pipeline line worth $27.31 per ADR that is a modelled return on R&D spend rather than a named asset, and that Brinsupri has to reach a $14bn to $20bn peak for the price to be justified, so we read SNY as fairly valued to modestly cheap rather than a Strong buy, pending the first full Brinsupri quarter inside Sanofi and the HSK31858 Phase 3 readout.

## 2. Investment thesis

1. **$41.0bn paid against $7.3bn of value by the book's method.** Insmed's three assets, forecast from patients and price with Sanofi's own cost ratios and 6.94% discount rate [Book inputs], carry a risk-adjusted value of $3.1bn low, $7.3bn base and $14.7bn high [Overlay]. Relaxing the cost ratios to those of a bought asset (cost of goods 15%, selling 20%, R&D 10%, other 5%) [Overlay assumption] lifts the base to $18.1bn. Both are less than half the price.

2. **Brinsupri has to peak at $14bn to $20bn for the price to work.** The breakeven peak is $20.5bn on the book's method and $14.2bn on acquired-asset margins [Overlay]. Our base is $5.1bn, built from 500,000 diagnosed US patients, 600,000 in the EU5 and 150,000 in Japan [Announced] with eligibility and penetration assumptions stated in section 5.

3. **The exclusivity gap is real and the deal fills about 30% of it in level.** The book's named-asset revenue falls EUR 19.9bn between 2031 and 2035 as Dupixent loses its US compound patent in March 2031 [Book, 20-F basis]. Insmed adds EUR 6.0bn to 6.6bn a year in those years in our risk-weighted base [Overlay]. Insmed's growth over 2032 to 2035 is EUR 0.6bn, so the gap is filled by level, not growth, and Brinsupri loses its own US composition patent on 12 March 2035 [Announced, Insmed 10-K].

4. **Accretion by end-2028 is a run-rate claim, not a full-year one.** On our base the deal is 10% dilutive to business EPS in 2027, 0.5% dilutive in 2028 and 5% accretive in 2029 [Overlay]. The company's words are "accretive to Sanofi's business earnings per share by the end of 2028" [Announced]. The claim holds in base on an exit run-rate and fails in the low case through 2032.

5. **Forty per cent of the standalone value is a modelled line, not an asset.** The book's $68.17 includes $27.31 per ADR of "launches the model assumes", a return of 0.321 of revenue per R&D dollar with an eight-year lag [Book]. At half that rate the fair value is about $54.5 per ADR. The breakpoints tool says the price of $46.77 needs only a productivity of 0.115 [Book].

6. **Dupixent is 28% of the price and the oral STAT6 threat is now data, not theory.** Dupixent carries $13.20 per ADR [Book]. Kymera's KT-621 cleared EASI-75 in 62% of patients against 22% on placebo at week 16 in a 244-patient Phase 2b [Announced, Kymera 8-K], with Phase 3 planned by mid-2027. A bear path for Dupixent of 10% growth, 2030 exclusivity and 40% first-year erosion costs 31% of its value, or $4.13 per ADR [Book what-if].

### Dupixent in depth

Dupixent explains 28.2% of what the company costs today [Book]. The book grows FY2025 revenue of EUR 15,714m at 20.2%, fading to nil over five years, to a peak of EUR 27,670m in 2030, and erodes it from 2031 at the biologic default of a 25% first-year fall [Book]. The exclusivity date is the US compound patent with patent term extension, March 2031 [Book, 20-F basis]; the 20-F itself gives Europe September 2032 with SPC (March 2033 with paediatric extension), Japan May 2034, later-filed patents pending to 2045, and US regulatory exclusivity to March 2029 [Announced, 20-F]. Q3 2026 sales were EUR 5,687m, up 34.8%, with US sales of EUR 4,376m up 40.2% [Announced].

The threat: Kymera's BROADEN2 Phase 2b read out on 23 November 2026 [Announced, Kymera 8-K exhibit 99.1]. In 244 adults and adolescents, the mid dose of oral KT-621 cut EASI by 72% against 27% on placebo at week 16 (p less than 0.0001), with EASI-75 in 62% against 22%, vIGA-AD 0/1 in 38% against 11%, and a four-point itch response in 52% against 13%. Conjunctivitis was 13.1% on the mid dose, all mild or moderate. The first Phase 3 in atopic dermatitis is planned by mid-2027 and an asthma Phase 2b reads out late 2027. The figures are from Kymera's 8-K, not from the pulse.

### The future pipeline line

The "launches the model assumes" line is $27.31 per ADR, 40% of the fair value [Book]. It is the pooled return on Sanofi's R&D spend, 0.321 of revenue per R&D dollar with an eight-year lag, applied over a twelve-year exclusivity term [Book]. The breakpoints tool says the fair value falls to the $46.77 price when that rate falls to 0.115 [Book], which is a straight line: at half the rate, 0.161, the line is worth about $13.65 and the fair value about $54.5 per ADR. The book also reports that 20.5% of the strip rests on assumption [Book, forecast board]. It prices Sanofi's R&D at the industry's historical yield, and the Insmed purchase spends the capacity it assumes without entering the R&D the rate divides by [Book, future_pipeline notes]. We carry it at half in the bear case.

## 5. Insmed's assets, as overlay

Method, the book's: patients times net price gives revenue; Sanofi's FY2025 cost ratios (cost of goods 27.9%, selling 20.4%, R&D 16.8%, other 13.9%, tax 15.66%) [Book] turn revenue into free cash flow; cash flows are discounted at the book's 6.94% [Book] to end-2026 with the book's post-exclusivity tail of 2.35 times the final year [Book convention]; each unapproved layer is weighted by the book's published base rates [Book]. Erosion after exclusivity uses the book's measured defaults: for a small molecule, Part D cost kept of 29.9%, 19.2%, 13.8%, 11.5%, 7.8% and 6.2% in the six years after first generic entry; for a biologic-like product, a 26.5% first-year fall and 25.2% a year after [Book erosion bases]. The one departure from the book: Brinsupri is anchored so that every case passes through FY2026E revenue of $1,349m, which is the nine months reported ($917.1m) plus a fourth quarter at the third quarter's $400.1m grown 8% [Announced plus assumption].

### Brinsupri (brensocatib)

Label: oral DPP1 inhibitor "indicated for the treatment of non-cystic fibrosis bronchiectasis (NCFB) in adult and pediatric patients 12 years of age and older", 10 mg or 25 mg once daily, no boxed warning, warnings for dermatologic and periodontal reactions [Announced, DailyMed label revised 3/2026]. FDA approval 12 August 2025, EU approval 18 November 2025, Japan approval 24 August 2026 [Announced, Insmed releases]. Evidence: ASPEN (NCT04594369, 1,721 randomised in the label population) cut the annualised exacerbation rate to 1.02 and 1.04 on 10 mg and 25 mg from 1.29 on placebo, rate ratios 0.79 (0.68 to 0.92) and 0.81 (0.69 to 0.94), with FEV1 falling 24 mL on 25 mg against 62 mL on placebo at week 52 [Announced, label and Insmed's NEJM release of 23 April 2025]. The NEJM paper itself could not be opened. The expansion indications are gone: BiRCh in chronic rhinosinusitis missed and was discontinued in December 2025, and CEDAR in hidradenitis suppurativa was terminated [Announced, 10-K and registry]. Competitors: HSK31858 (Haisco) is in Phase 3 with primary completion 28 November 2026 (NCT06660992); BI 1291583 (Boehringer) is in Phase 3 AIRTIVITY with primary completion 15 June 2027 (NCT06872892) [Announced, ClinicalTrials.gov]. The book's bronchiectasis landscape (indication 285) lists no DPP1 inhibitor: its three universe candidates are Bayer's inhaled ciprofloxacin, GSK3862995B in Phase 2 and AstraZeneca's AZD0292 in Phase 2 [Book]. Exclusivity: the US composition patent US 9,522,894 expires 12 March 2035; later patents run to 2039 and 2040; a patent term extension is not stated [Announced, 10-K]. Net price: no primary source for the list price was opened, so it is an assumption.

## 9. Risks

- **Deal completion.** The vote needs more than two thirds of votes entitled to be cast, a higher bar than a simple majority; the termination fee is $1.125bn, 2.7% of equity value [Announced]. The agreement carries no financing condition and a bridge mandate dated 22 November [Announced]. We put completion odds high but the book has no tool for them: no free data.
- **Antitrust.** Sanofi has no DPP1 inhibitor and no inhaled prostanoid; the overlap is respiratory in the broad sense. HSR and foreign direct investment clearances are the conditions [Announced]. The end date can run to February 2028 if only regulatory conditions remain [Announced].
- **Integration.** Insmed had 1,664 employees at end-2025 [Announced, 10-K, summary only]; Ayvakit already carries "higher government rebates because of the originator Blueprint becoming part of Sanofi" [Announced, Q3 release].
- **Brinsupri uptake.** The base needs 30% of eligible US patients at $70,000 net. The 2026 run-rate is $1.6bn a quarter annualised [Announced]; the slope after 2027 is the whole case, and two DPP1 rivals read out within seven months. The low case is a plateau near $1.8bn.
- **Dupixent erosion.** An oral with Dupixent-like efficacy from 2029 to 2030, if Kymera's Phase 3 starts mid-2027 and succeeds, would take the Dupixent value down 31% on the book's own arithmetic, $4.13 per ADR [Book what-if].
- **Leverage.** 2.8x to 3.1x at close [Overlay]; the company says it "expects to maintain a strong credit rating" [Announced]. A second large deal before 2029 would test that.
- **Pricing policy.** Dupixent "is not currently expected to be impacted by IRA until 2031" [Announced, 20-F]; the book finds no CMS-selected Sanofi drug [Book]. Brinsupri, an oral with 2035 patents, is a negotiation candidate in the early 2030s; we have not modelled it.
